Online Multi-Workflow Scheduling under
Uncertain Task Execution Time in IaaS Clouds
Jiagang Liu, Ju Ren, Member, IEEE, Wei Dai, Deyu Zhang, Member, IEEE, Pude Zhou,
Yaoxue Zhang, Senior Member, IEEE, Geyong Min, Member, IEEE, Noushin Najjari
Abstract— Cloud has become an important platform for executing numerous deadline-constrained scientific applications generally
represented by workflow models. It provides scientists a simple and cost-efficient method of running workflows on their rental Virtual
Machines (VMs) anytime and anywhere. Since pay-as-you-go is a dominating pricing solution in clouds, extensive research efforts
have been devoted to minimizing the monetary cost of executing workflows by designing tailored VM allocation mechanisms. However,
most of them assume that the task execution time in clouds is static and can be estimated in advance, which is impractical in real
scenarios due to performance fluctuation of VMs. In this paper, we propose an onliNe multi-workflOw Scheduling Framework, named
NOSF, to schedule deadline-constrained workflows with random arrivals and uncertain task execution time. In NOSF, workflow
scheduling process consists of three phases, including workflow preprocessing, VM allocation and feedback process. Built upon the
new framework, a deadline-aware heuristic algorithm is then developed to elastically provision suitable VMs for workflow execution,
with the objective of minimizing the rental cost and improving resource utilization. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed
algorithm significantly outperforms two state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of reducing VM rental costs and deadline violation
probability, as well as improving the resource utilization efficiency.
Index Terms—Cloud Computing, Workflow Scheduling, VM Rental Cost, Uncertain Task Execution Time, Multiple Workflows.

expenses on different VMs are ignored in our model.

In real cloud environments, VMs are incapable of maintaining
persistent computing capabilities. Hence, the execution time of
a task on a VM can fluctuate along with the variation of the
computing capability of the VM. In the case, modeling of per-
formance fluctuations of VMs can be transformed into a problem
of modeling the task execution time with stochastic characteristics.
The assumption that the task execution time is subject to normal
distributions has been employed by many researchers [10], [28],
[29]. In our work, the task execution time on a VM is also
regarded as stochastic variables with independent and normal
distributions.The expected value and variance of task execution
time can be known by using some typical performance estimation
techniques, such as historic tables [30], statistical prediction [31]
and analytical modeling [32], in advance.

6.3.2 Deadline Violation Probability
In this subsection, we evaluate the deadline violation probability
of the three algorithms for scheduling a medium template set.
Fig. 7 shows that all three algorithms have a few workflows
that do not complete before the deadlines under fluctuated task
execution time. We can see that the deadline violation probability
of IC-PCPD2 is much higher than the results of NOSF and
ROSA because IC-PCPD2 fails to deal with the influence of
the fluctuated execution time. Specifically, from Figs. 7(a) and
7(b), we can observe that NOSF is obviously less than ROSA,
in terms of the average duration of exceeding the deadlines and
the number of workflows unsatisfying the deadlines, when α
increases. This can be interpreted that the feedback process in
NOSF controls the accumulation of the fluctuated task execution
time for mitigating its propagation, so that the deadline violation
probability is reduced. Figs. 7(c) and 7(d) reveal that the deadline
violation probabilities of the three algorithms decrease with the
increment of β. This demonstrates that prolongation of deadline
enlarges the slack of tasks, so that each of the algorithms can
schedule more workflows satisfying the relevant deadlines.

6.3.4 Performance of Scheduling Large Workflows
In this subsection, we evaluate the experimental results of the three
algorithms for scheduling workflows from the large template set.
As shown in Fig. 9, we can obviously see that the performance
of NOSF for scheduling large workflows is still much better than
IC-PCPD2 and ROSA in terms of the total VM rental cost and
the utilization efficiency of leasing VMs, as well as the deadline
violation probability. Besides, Fig. 9(a) shows that the total VM
rental cost of each algorithm for scheduling large workflows is
much higher than that for scheduling workflows from the medium
template set. It is obvious that the more tasks among a workflow
need longer time that leased VMs take to execute them, which
results in higher costs. Moreover, Fig. 9(b) indicates that the
number of tasks in workflows has little impact on resource uti-
lization of scheduling multiple workflows. Additionally, Fig. 9(c)
shows that the number of missed workflow deadlines is much
higher in each of the algorithms for scheduling large workflows
than that for scheduling workflows from the medium template set.
However, Fig. 9(d) reveals that the average duration of exceeding
the deadlines are much lower in each algorithm for scheduling
large workflows for the following reasons. On one hand, the
execution of the workflow containing more tasks results in the
